## 5. What happened at Liberty Jail?

Joseph Smith, his brother Hyrum, Sidney Rigdon, Lyman Wight, Caleb Baldwin, and Alexander McRae were imprisoned in a jail in Liberty, Missouri, on December 1, 1838, for crimes allegedly committed during conflicts with other Missourians. They had surrendered after Missouri governor Lilburn W. Boggs ordered that the Saints be driven from the state or exterminated. An initial hearing found sufficient evidence against them, leading to their detention in the Clay County Jail in Liberty until their trial in late spring 1839. The prisoners pursued legal means to obtain their freedom and attempted to escape on at least two occasions. Eventually, on April 6, 1839, an armed guard escorted them from the jail, and during the transport, the guards allowed them to escape, enabling them to make their way to Illinois.

## 6. What was Zion's Camp?

Zion's Camp, also known as the Camp of Israel, was organized in 1834 under the direction of Joseph Smith. It was initially envisioned as a military operation to march to Missouri and request the governor, Daniel Dunklin, to call up the state militia to restore the Saints to their lands. The camp was intended to be a defensive measure to protect Church members from being driven out again (Church History Topics: Zion’s Camp). However, a revelation received on June 22, 1834, indicated that it was no longer required for the camp to redeem Zion, and the elders needed to receive an endowment of power in the Kirtland house of the Lord before any further action could take place (Church History Topics: Zion’s Camp). The camp began to disband at the end of June, and many members returned to Kirtland, especially after a cholera epidemic affected the camp (Church History Topics: Zion’s Camp).

## 8. What was the Kirtland Temple dedication like?

The dedication of the Kirtland Temple on March 27, 1836, was a significant and spiritually uplifting event for the Saints. They gathered to partake of the sacrament and listen to several sermons. Joseph Smith offered a dedicatory prayer that he had received by revelation, which is recorded in Doctrine and Covenants 109. Following the prayer, the Saints expressed their joy through the Hosanna Shout and sang “The Spirit of God like a Fire Is Burning,” a hymn specifically written for this occasion by William W. Phelps. Many attendees reported experiencing great spiritual manifestations, including the sound of a rushing mighty wind, speaking in tongues, visions, and the visitation of angels. This dedication set a pattern for future temple dedications, with elements like the dedicatory prayer and the Hosanna Shout becoming standard practices in subsequent ceremonies (Church History Topics: Temple Dedications and Dedicatory Prayers; Church History Topics: Kirtland Temple).

## 9. Who was Emma Smith?

Emma Smith, born on July 10, 1804, in Willingsborough, Pennsylvania, was the wife of Joseph Smith and played a significant role in the restoration of the Church. She was praised by her mother-in-law, Lucy Mack Smith, for her courage, zeal, and patience in enduring hardships and persecution throughout her life (Church History Topics: Emma Hale Smith). Emma was also the first president of the Relief Society, which was organized in March 1842 by Joseph Smith (Gospel Topics: Relief Society). Despite her complicated feelings about plural marriage, she remained deeply committed to Joseph Smith (Gospel Topics: Plural Marriage). Later in life, she maintained her belief in Joseph's prophetic role and the divine authenticity of the Book of Mormon (Church History Topics: Emma Hale Smith — Later Years).

## 10. What is the Word of Wisdom's history?

The Word of Wisdom is a revelation given by the Lord to the Prophet Joseph Smith in 1833, which is now recorded in Doctrine and Covenants 89. This revelation teaches us about healthy practices, encouraging the consumption of grains, fruits, and herbs while advising against substances like tobacco, alcohol, tea, and coffee. Initially, it was given “not by commandment or constraint” (Doctrine and Covenants 89:2), but over time, Church leaders have emphasized that obeying the Word of Wisdom is essential for receiving the full blessings of the restored gospel, including baptism, missionary service, and temple ordinances.

The historical context of the Word of Wisdom helps us understand its significance. The principles of the Word of Wisdom have evolved, with leaders providing guidance on various substances over time, emphasizing the importance of using wisdom and prayerful judgment in health-related choices.

## 11. Why are there several accounts of the First Vision?

Joseph Smith left multiple accounts of the First Vision to provide a more complete picture of this miraculous experience. Each account emphasizes different aspects depending on the audience and the purpose for sharing. This is similar to how different scriptural accounts, such as Paul's conversion, highlight various details while maintaining a consistent story. The variations in Joseph's accounts are expected and do not indicate fabrication; rather, they enrich our understanding of the event (Gospel Topics).

The first account states that "The Lord opened the heavens upon me and I saw the Lord and he spake unto me," emphasizing the central role of Jesus Christ in the vision (Gospel Topics). The different accounts allow us to appreciate Joseph's sincere testimony that God answered his prayer with this remarkable vision (Gospel Topics).

## 15. Hi! What did Emma Smith do for the Church's hymns?

Emma Smith played a significant role in the selection and publication of hymns for the Church. She assisted Joseph Smith as a scribe during the early stages of the Book of Mormon translation and began selecting hymns to be sung in church meetings. Working with W. W. Phelps, she helped print some hymns in Church newspapers as early as 1832, a time when male clergy typically assumed responsibility for hymn selection. The first Latter-day Saint hymnbook, which was printed in Kirtland in 1835, was published under her name and contained 90 hymns, with many written by Latter-day Saints and others being popular Protestant hymns. Emma's second hymnal, printed in Nauvoo in 1841, included 304 hymns. These early hymnbooks contained only the words of the hymns, with familiar tunes selected for singing.
